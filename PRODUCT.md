# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three overlapping audiences, all equally valid:

- **Galleries & curators** — art world professionals assessing whether to show or acquire. They need credentials, quality of documentation, and a sense of the artist's range and seriousness.
- **Private collectors** — individuals looking to buy or commission. They need to feel desire and trust simultaneously.
- **General public & press** — journalists, students, anyone discovering the work. They need to feel welcome and curious, not intimidated.

All three arrive from different doors but need the same thing: to slow down and look carefully.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio site for Italian sculptor Robert Mariotti. The site exists to surface his work to the broadest possible art-interested audience — collectors, curators, press, and the curious public — and to communicate the depth, range, and character of his practice without over-explaining it.

Success looks like a visitor who lingers, looks at multiple pieces, and leaves feeling they've encountered something genuinely surprising. Secondary success: a gallery or collector who reaches out.

## Brand Personality

Elegant, refined, Italian.

The brand carries European restraint — craft over spectacle, proportion over decoration, the object over the caption. Warmth comes through the work itself and through Robert's presence as a person (his portrait, his voice in the text), not through design flourishes.

Emotional goal: **awe and curiosity**. The site should feel like it was made with the same care and attention as the sculptures.

## Anti-references

- **Busy / decorative** — no textures, flourishes, or visual noise competing with the work. Every design element must earn its place.
- Generic artist portfolio templates (beige/serif defaults, whitecube clones, anything that could belong to any artist).

## Design Principles

1. **The work is the hero.** The site's job is to get out of the way. Frames, space, and restraint exist to direct attention to the sculpture, not to the interface.
2. **European restraint.** Elegance through proportion and absence. Nothing should be added that doesn't materially improve the experience.
3. **Curiosity over completeness.** Leave visitors wanting to know more — about the piece, about the artist, about what comes next. The site is an invitation, not a catalogue.
4. **Material intelligence.** The site should feel like it was made with the same craft as the objects it presents. Considered details, nothing sloppy or default.
5. **Time well spent.** Slow the visitor down. This is not a scroll-and-bounce experience. Spacing, pacing, and image treatment should reward careful looking.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance. Keyboard navigation, sufficient contrast, screen reader basics. Reduced motion support for any animations added.
